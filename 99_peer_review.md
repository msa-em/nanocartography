This article was first submitted to Microscopy and Microanalysis, where it received the two referee reports reproduced below.
While the manuscript was waiting for a third referee, the Microscopy and Microanalysis editors suggested publishing it in Elemental Microscopy instead.
Neither referee requested major changes, and the authors addressed all of the referee comments in this version of the article.
Both referees are anonymous.

## First Report

### Reviewer 1

:::{note} Reviewer Comment
I want to thank the authors for this excellent article. It was well written, and easy to follow (with the exception of the very complex mathematical logic throughout).

The methodologies were very carefully considered and documented including multiple examples for how this tool could be utilize for more efficient utilization of electron microscopes and experimental planning. Having this methodology as a required training for early career microscopy students/staff would benefit the microscopy community with respect to responsible stewardship of microscopy facilities.

A few recommendations to help correct some editorial errors:

- figure caption and figures on different pages,
- equation caption and equation on different pages,
- (page20 "insert inline documentation here" comment seems overlooked, etc.
- This article would benefit from a final editorial scrub.
:::

:::{tip} Author Response
The authors addressed all of these comments in this version of the article.
:::

### Reviewer 2

:::{note} Reviewer Comment
The manuscript entitled "Nanocartography: Planning for success in analytical electron microscopy" describes a promising approach to facilitate navigating through crystalline samples in TEMs. It is very extensive and provides substantial information on the methods and background theory used. This topic is worth investigating and, if implemented successfully, it'd provide with a long needed tool to facility any diffraction-related work or interface-analysis in the TEM.

As mentioned above, I believe this is a very necessary and timely topic. Someone needs to take the initiative, since the EM manufacturers are not, and the authors have done an excellent work that it's worth praising. My main issue (apart from small typos listed below) is that it is excessively long, borderline with textbook style. There are many sections that just cover the theory that anyone with a science background should be familiar with. I appreciate they are particularly applied to this issue but I'm concern that they will detract potential users which don't have the time/motivation to reach the end of the paper. The main emphasis should be on how useful this approach can be and how "easy" to implement it could be if the community agrees on a protocol.

My suggestion is turn section 2 into an appendix and greatly reduce section 3 by moving most of the mathematical work to another appendix. As mentioned earlier, most of the maths are relatively straight forward and just divert from the main point the paper tries to make.

There are some changes to make by the authors:

- Hexagonal notation: The authors use a 3-index notation for hexagonal directions, while the most widely used is the 4-index one. I suggest this is changed.
- end of p27: F is not 0 or 1, it can take other values
- eq 38: + not *
:::

:::{tip} Author Response
The authors addressed all of these comments in this version of the article. Three-index notation is kept for hexagonal directions for ease of comparison with the cubic system, as noted in the text.
:::
